Analogy: AI Tools and “Vibe” Coders

Reflecting on integrity, creativity, and skill level in AI-assisted development.

Would I agree to the ₱40,000 wedding documentation deal?

In the scenario, the documentation team charges ₱40,000 for a wedding package. They will use an iPhone 16 Pro Max to cover the event and CapCut for the same-day edit (SDE). The team has two years of experience and an above-average portfolio.

Personally, I might agree to the deal, but only after checking a few important details. Expensive equipment and trendy apps do not automatically guarantee quality. What matters more are:

  • The consistency and style of their previous outputs
  • How they handle lighting, framing, and storytelling
  • Reviews from past clients
  • Their professionalism, backup plans, and contract terms

If their portfolio shows genuine skill in capturing emotions, clean editing, and reliable performance under pressure, then the use of an iPhone and CapCut is not a problem. Modern smartphones and editing apps are very capable when used by skilled professionals. In that case, the ₱40,000 might be worth it because I am paying for their eye, timing, and creativity, not just the tools.

However, if their portfolio feels inconsistent, overly dependent on flashy filters, or lacks storytelling, then the price would not feel justified. Tools can make the output look “okay,” but they cannot replace experience, discipline, and artistic judgment.

Integrity of their work

The integrity of the documentation team’s work is not measured by how “high-tech” their tools are, but by:

  • Whether they are honest about what they can and cannot do
  • How clearly they explain the inclusions and limitations of the package
  • How much effort they put into planning, shooting, and editing
  • Whether they deliver on time and match the quality they promised

If they openly say, “We use a phone and CapCut, but here is the quality we consistently produce,” and the output matches the price, then their work still has integrity. They are transparent and confident in their craft, not pretending to be something they are not.

Connecting the analogy to “vibe” coders

In the tech world, some developers are sometimes called “vibe” coders. They rely heavily on AI tools and code generators, and they may look productive on the surface: they can paste snippets, generate UIs, or fix bugs quickly. Similar to the wedding team using an iPhone and CapCut, these “vibe” coders are powered by modern tools.

The real question is not whether they use AI, but:

  • Do they understand the code they ship?
  • Can they debug problems without blindly asking the AI again and again?
  • Do they respect security, performance, and maintainability?
  • Are they honest about which parts are AI-generated and which are their own work?

Integrity, creativity, and skill level of AI-assisted (“vibe”) coders

Integrity: A developer who uses AI ethically is similar to an honest wedding team. They acknowledge that they use tools like ChatGPT or Copilot, review the outputs, and take responsibility for the final result. A developer with low integrity simply copy-pastes whatever AI suggests, claims full credit, and does not care about consequences when bugs appear.

Creativity: AI can suggest patterns, templates, and examples, but the unique “flavor” of a project still comes from the human. Just like a videographer chooses which moments to highlight, a developer decides how features fit together, how the user experience should feel, and how to solve real user problems. AI can assist, but it cannot fully replace that creative decision-making.

Skill level: A strong developer who uses AI will:

  • Understand the underlying concepts (data structures, algorithms, architecture).
  • Read and modify AI-generated code to match project standards.
  • Identify when AI output is wrong, insecure, or inefficient.

A weak “vibe” coder, on the other hand, becomes lost when the AI output fails. They may not know how to debug, test, or refactor. Their skill is limited to prompt-and-paste, similar to a wedding team that only knows how to apply filters but cannot handle bad lighting or unexpected events.

Final reflection

Tools like iPhones, CapCut, and Generative AI are powerful, but they do not define professionalism by themselves. What truly matters is:

  • Integrity – being honest, responsible, and transparent about tool usage.
  • Creativity – using tools to enhance imagination, not to hide a lack of ideas.
  • Skill – continuously learning the fundamentals so that tools become amplifiers, not crutches.

Whether in wedding documentation or software development, AI should not be an excuse to lower standards. Instead, it should be a partner that helps skilled, ethical people do their best work.
