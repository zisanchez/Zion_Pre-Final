Ethical Use of AI

Guidelines for responsible use of Generative AI in academe, research, and daily life.

Responsible Use in the Academe

In school, GenAI should be treated as a learning assistant, not as a shortcut to avoid thinking.

  • Use AI for brainstorming topics, outlines, and examples.
  • Ask AI to explain complex concepts in simpler language.
  • Compare AI outputs with your notes, books, or reliable sources.
  • Follow your school’s AI policy and your instructor’s specific rules.

The goal is to enhance your learning, not to outsource it.

Avoiding Plagiarism & Misuse

Plagiarism happens when you present someone else’s work—including AI output—as your own without proper acknowledgment.

  • Do not copy-paste AI-generated essays or code and submit as original.
  • Rewrite, analyze, and add your own ideas and reflections.
  • When AI significantly contributes, mention it in your methodology or acknowledgments.
  • Never use AI to generate fake data, fake citations, or fabricated results.

Ethical students are transparent about the tools they use.

Accuracy & Verification of AI Outputs

GenAI can sound very confident even when it is wrong.

  • Cross-check key facts with textbooks, journals, and official websites.
  • Verify statistics, quotations, and citations from original sources.
  • Be skeptical of medical, legal, or financial advice from AI tools.
  • If something sounds “too perfect” or suspicious, double-check it.

Treat AI outputs as drafts or starting points, not final truth.

Bias & Fairness

AI systems learn from data that may contain historical biases and stereotypes. As a result, their outputs might be unfair.

  • Watch out for sexist, racist, or discriminatory language in outputs.
  • Do not use AI to label or judge people based on sensitive traits.
  • Design prompts that are respectful and inclusive.
  • Remember: you are responsible for how you use AI-generated content.

Ethical AI use respects the dignity and rights of all people.

Recommended Guidelines for Students

  1. Be transparent. If allowed, mention when and how you used AI in your work.
  2. Think first, then prompt. Try to understand the topic before asking AI for help.
  3. Use AI as a tutor, not a ghostwriter. Let it explain, not replace, your own effort.
  4. Protect privacy. Avoid sharing sensitive personal data or confidential information.
  5. Respect copyright. Do not claim AI-generated images, music, or text as purely your own art without context.
  6. Stay within policies. Follow your school’s academic integrity rules and AI guidelines.
  7. Keep learning. Use AI to improve your skills, not to skip practice.
