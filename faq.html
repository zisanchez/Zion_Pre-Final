Frequently Asked Questions about Generative AI

Beginner-friendly explanations for students, researchers, and curious users.

Generative AI is a type of artificial intelligence that can create new content after being trained on large datasets. It can generate:
  • Text (e.g., essays, summaries, code, chat responses)
  • Images (e.g., artwork, logos, concept designs)
  • Audio (e.g., music, voice synthesis)
  • Video or animations
It does this by learning patterns and then predicting what output would be most likely given a prompt.

Most modern GenAI models use deep learning, especially neural networks called transformers. During training, the model is exposed to huge amounts of example data (text, images, code, etc.). It learns patterns like grammar, structure, relationships between words or pixels, and common sequences.

When you give it a prompt, the model predicts the next word (for text) or pixel pattern (for images) step by step. It does not “understand” content like a human, but it is very good at pattern matching.

Some popular GenAI tools include:
  • Text & chat: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Copilot
  • Images: DALL·E, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, Canva AI
  • Code assistants: GitHub Copilot, CodeWhisperer, Cursor
  • Video & audio: Runway, Synthesia, ElevenLabs
New tools appear regularly, so it’s important to check the terms of service and ethical guidelines of each tool you use.

GenAI is strong at:
  • Brainstorming ideas and outlines
  • Drafting emails, essays, and reports
  • Generating code snippets or helping debug
  • Summarizing long texts or lectures
  • Creating visual concepts, thumbnails, and designs
  • Language translation and explanation of concepts
It is most helpful when you use it as a support tool—not as a full replacement for your own thinking.

Limitations and risks include:
  • Hallucinations: The model can generate confident but false information.
  • Bias: Outputs may reflect stereotypes or biases present in the training data.
  • Plagiarism risk: Overreliance on AI text/image/code can lead to academic dishonesty.
  • Privacy: Sensitive personal data should not be pasted into AI tools.
  • Overdependence: Relying on AI for everything can weaken your own skills.
This is why verification, critical thinking, and ethical guidelines are essential.

It depends on your teacher’s or school’s policy. In general:
  • It is usually okay to use GenAI for brainstorming, idea generation, and explanations.
  • It becomes unethical if you submit AI-generated work as if it were fully your own without disclosure.
  • You should cite or acknowledge GenAI tools when they significantly contributed to your work.
  • When in doubt, ask your instructor how much AI assistance is allowed.

GenAI will change many jobs, but in most cases it will act as a copilot or assistant rather than a total replacement. Human skills—like critical thinking, ethics, creativity, and emotional intelligence—remain essential.

The people who benefit most are those who know how to use AI tools responsibly and still keep learning and practicing their own skills.

Some practical tips:
  • Start with simple prompts like “Explain X to me like I’m a beginner.”
  • Use it to review lessons, summarize readings, and test your understanding.
  • Compare AI explanations with your notes and textbooks.
  • Always double-check important information from reliable sources.
  • Practice writing your own answers first, then ask AI to critique or improve them.
